# Two rotation actions and the missing relativistic bridge

November 8, 2026. Exploratory / Not Promoted. Standard symmetry algebra applied to the explicitly conditional internal sector.

## Question

Once an exact internal SU(2) exists and N=1 is chosen, can it become spatial spin simply by declaring it to be the rotation group? There is a precise construction to test, and a precise distinction between its mathematical availability and its physical selection.

## 1 Product symmetry and a possible diagonal action

Let L_i generate the original spatial rotations of the geometric data, and let S_i be the exact internal oscillator generators in the chosen sector. Suppose both are symmetries and commute, as in the geometry-independent separated Origin–Authorship specialization. In hbar=1 units,

\[
[L_i,L_j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}L_k,\quad
[S_i,S_j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}S_k,\quad [L_i,S_j]=0.
\]

Both L_i and J_i=L_i+S_i obey the rotation algebra. If they commute with the Hamiltonian individually, so does their sum. Thus there is an available diagonal Spin(3) action inside the product of spatial and internal symmetries. This is a constructive possibility, not an absence of every rotation representation.

Within the limited homogeneous mixing family L_i+c S_i,

\[
[L_i+cS_i,L_j+cS_j]
-i\epsilon_{ijk}(L_k+cS_k)
=i\epsilon_{ijk}(c^2-c)S_k.
\]

Closure requires c=0 or c=1 for a nontrivial internal representation. The original scalar assignment and the diagonal assignment are both algebraically consistent at this level. Conservation alone does not choose between them. This is not a classification of all possible embeddings or nonlinear generators.

The exact matrices in `check_rotation_lift.py` compare an orbital l=1 representation tensored with the internal doublet. A 2 pi rotation generated by L is plus identity; the chosen diagonal generator gives minus identity. Its total Casimir splits into j=1/2 and j=3/2 multiplets, as expected from the conventional tensor-product construction. The calculation checks the distinction; it does not select the diagonal action as physical.

The fixed-geometry nonlinear dark-mode pair has an internal symmetry but need not have the full product spacetime symmetry required by this argument. A prescribed background and a joint spacetime-invariant model must not be conflated.

## 2 Why this does not evade the reconstruction obstruction

The original identity labels are specified as scalar internal coordinates. A full spatial rotation therefore acts trivially on those labels. The diagonal action changes that assignment: its central element acts as minus identity on the canonical oscillator amplitudes. It changes the reconstruction domain and its group action, which is exactly the extra structure required to escape the revised GWFM's spin-equivariance obstruction.

The action can possess both transformations as symmetries without identifying them as the same physical operation. To choose the diagonal one, the theory must specify how apparatus orientations and measurements couple to its internal generators. A transformation of an isolated internal oscillator is not yet a measured spatial rotation.

There is an observable issue as well. In the current identity model, the scalar observable F=sum I_a is an explicitly chosen contraction. The central internal transformation flips the selected pair coordinates and momenta. It generally changes F. Declaring that internal phase transformation to be a gauge identification would therefore require revising which identity observables are physical. A global symmetry is not automatically a gauge redundancy.

On a chosen N=1 quantum sector, the central transformation is a common phase on state vectors, so it acts trivially on rays. But obtaining that sector and its quantum measurement interpretation is an additional construction, not a consequence of the original real-coordinate observable.

## 3 Keeping the original boosts fails

A relativistic spin assignment must fit the Lorentz algebra, not just its rotation subalgebra. Suppose the original boost generators K_i act on the tensorial geometric data and trivially on the scalar internal labels. Their algebra includes

\[
[K_i,K_j]=-i\epsilon_{ijk}L_k.
\]

If physical rotations are now assigned to J=L+S while those boosts are left unchanged, the Lorentz relation would instead require -i epsilon_ijk (L_k+S_k). The mismatch is i epsilon_ijk S_k and is nonzero on a doublet. The exact matrix comparison records this failure.

Consequently the diagonal rotation assignment alone is not a relativistic theory. A consistent new boost action, constraints, energy-momentum relation and physical state space would be required. Standard massive-particle representation theory supplies ways to build such structures given mass and little-group spin as inputs; using it here would not show that those inputs emerged from SEFI.

This calculation does not construct the canonical boosts of the higher-derivative worldline model. It states the algebraic mismatch under the stated original scalar-label action and unchanged boosts. The prior worldline energy obstruction also remains unresolved.

## 4 What the next successful step would require

The audit now distinguishes two missing inputs rather than treating them as one:

1. A justified rule selecting the internal doublet; the current Hamiltonian conserves all N sectors and does not make N=1 the global lowest excitation in the tested nonlinear example.
2. A physical rotation and boost assignment on that selected state space, with an observation law and consistent relativistic dynamics.

A proposed completion must state its new assumptions, demonstrate closure of the full spacetime symmetry/constraint algebra, and retain positive physical energy. Only then would exchange statistics, electromagnetic coupling and quantitative electron tests become meaningful for that completion.

The existing model has not reached physical matter or an electron–positron field. These notes identify concrete missing mathematics. They do not license adding the known electron representation and then describing it as an emergent prediction.
